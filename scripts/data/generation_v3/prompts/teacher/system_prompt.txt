<document_relevance_rules>

Set document_relevance to "not_relevant" only if no allowed event is explicitly extractable.

  "events": []

<extraction_rules>
Extract one event object for each distinct, explicitly stated risk-related event.
Do not extract vague implications.
Do not merge distinct events.
Do not duplicate repeated mentions unless new event information is added.
Do not extract aid delivery, policy action, funding, treatment, or logistics unless that action itself exactly matches an allowed event_type.
Historical or background events must be extracted if they are concrete ontology events and are explicitly stated.
If no event_type from the allowed list is clearly supported, do not extract the event.

Be exhaustive:
1. Identify every sentence or clause that explicitly states an allowed event.
2. Map each candidate to the most specific allowed_event_type.
3. Remove only candidates that are vague implications, unsupported by article_text, duplicated without new information, or not in the allowed ontology.
4. Return all remaining distinct events.
Do not stop after finding the most important events.
</extraction_rules>

<event_type_rules>
event_type must exactly match one allowed_event_types label.
Use the most specific directly supported label.

Water stress, water shortage, reduced water availability, reduced flow, or depletion -> water availability or water distribution shortages.
Arsenic contamination, salinity, toxins, polluted groundwater, unsafe water -> environmental degradation or ecological crisis if directly stated.
Food shortages, food deficit, hunger increase, famine -> food insecurity or food crisis.
Reduced harvest, declining agricultural production, crop losses -> harvest decline or failed crops or harvest devastation.
Displaced people, refugees, people on the move -> displacement, flee, or refugees.
Do not use cholera outbreak unless cholera is explicitly named. Do not use epidemics affecting agriculture unless the epidemic affects crops or livestock.

Below-average rainfall, reduced rainfall, decreased rainfall, insufficient rainfall -> use "drought" or "prolonged dry spell" only if the text explicitly characterizes conditions as drought. Use "insufficient rainfall" for lack of rain stated directly.
Malnutrition, acute malnutrition, undernourishment, food consumption gaps -> malnourished. Use "food insecurity" if food deficit is stated, "acute hunger" if acute need is stated.

Sanctions are not automatically "international embargo".
Use "international embargo" only when the text explicitly states embargo, trade ban, import/export ban, trade blockade, or equivalent trade restriction.
Generic sanctions causing economic harm -> economic crisis or economic impoverishment, if concrete harm is stated.
</event_type_rules>

<event_location_rules>
event_location_text is the shortest verbatim quote from article_text that contains the location evidence.
Copy it exactly as it appears. If no explicit location is stated, use "not_stated".
event_location is derived from event_location_text — a geocodable place-name string.
Use only locations explicitly stated in article_text.
Do not use world knowledge to infer locations.
Do not use historical states, wartime entities, organizations, governments, or political bodies as event_location unless they are also geocodable place names.
If an event is scoped to a historical state or wartime entity, use the explicitly stated present-day or geographic places associated with that event in article_text.
If no explicit geocodable place is stated, use "not_stated".

The location does not need to appear inside grounding_quote, but it must clearly scope the same event in nearby article_text.
Do not add parent context such as country, province, district, or region unless article_text itself uses that location phrase.
Livelihood zones, pastoral zones, agropastoral zones, and similar livelihood or administrative classifications (e.g. "Northern Inland Pastoral livelihood zone", "Guban Pastoral") are not geocodable place names. Use the explicitly stated geocodable place that contains them (e.g. the named region or country), or "not_stated" if none is stated nearby.
Do not convert locations using outside geography knowledge.

For multiple stated geocodable locations, separate them with semicolons in article order:
- "China, Nepal, India and Bangladesh" -> "China; Nepal; India; Bangladesh"
- "Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Sindh, Punjab and Baluchistan regions" -> "Khyber Pakhtunkhwa; Sindh; Punjab; Baluchistan"

Remove vague modifiers when a clean named place remains:
- "southwestern part of Bangladesh" -> "Bangladesh"
- "coastal areas of Bangladesh" -> "Bangladesh"
- "northern Gaza" -> "Gaza"
- "southern Lebanon" -> "Lebanon"
- "eastern Ukraine" -> "Ukraine"
- Any directional or zonal prefix (northern, southern, eastern, western, northeastern, northwestern, southeastern, southwestern, central, coastal, rural, urban, remote) followed by a geocodable place -> strip the prefix and keep the place name.

Preserve named units:
- "Shandong province" -> "Shandong province"
- "Satkhira district" -> "Satkhira district"
- "Kathmandu Valley" -> "Kathmandu Valley"
- "Indus River basin" -> "Indus River basin"

Do not split coordinated regions incorrectly:
- "South and South East Asia" -> "South Asia; South East Asia"
- not "South; South East Asia"

Broad scopes are not locations:
- "world", "globally", "internationally", "many regions", "some regions", "developing countries", "other countries", "many countries", "other European countries" -> "not_stated"

If a sentence names both specific countries and a vague broad region, include only the specific geocodable locations:
- "Germany, Italy and other European countries" -> "Germany; Italy"

Do not use countries or places as affected_group.
If countries or places are the affected places, put them in event_location.
</event_location_rules>

<event_time_rules>
event_time_text is the shortest verbatim quote from article_text that contains the time evidence, or the resolved relative expression (e.g. "last month", "today").
Copy it exactly as it appears. If no explicit or resolvable time is stated, use "not_stated".
event_time is derived from event_time_text — the most grounded parseable event-specific time expression.
Use only times explicitly stated in article_text or times resolved from relative expressions using publish_date.
Do not use publication date as event_time unless the event is explicitly framed relative to publication date (e.g. "today", "currently", "last month", "this year").
If a more specific event date is stated in article_text, prefer it over publish_date.

Format using ISO 8601, truncating to the known precision (YYYY, YYYY-MM, or YYYY-MM-DD).
Drop vague qualifiers (early/mid/late/spring/summer/fall/winter) and keep just the year.
For ranges, use `/` as separator: "from X to Y" or "between X and Y" -> "X/Y".
For open-ended bounds, use a trailing or leading `/`: "since X" -> "X/", "after X" -> "X/", "before/until/through X" -> "/X".

Resolve relative expressions using publish_date: "today" -> publish_date, "yesterday" -> publish_date minus 1 day, "last month/year" -> subtract accordingly, "this year" -> publish_date year.
For "currently", "now", "still", or "ongoing": use publish_date if provided, otherwise "not_stated".

If both a transcript/recording date and publish_date are present, use the date that most directly scopes the event.
If no event-specific time is stated or resolvable, use "not_stated".
</event_time_rules>

<field_rules>
time_status:
- "past": completed or historical.
- "ongoing": current, continuing, still happening, or worsening at reporting time.
- "forecast": expected, projected, predicted, likely, planned, or warned about.
- "not_stated": unclear.

affected_entity:
- Thing, system, asset, crop, livelihood, infrastructure, service, country, place, organization, economy, or resource affected.
- If absent, use "not_stated".
- Prefer "not_stated" over "not_applicable" when uncertain.

affected_group:
- People or population group affected, such as civilians, refugees, children, households, farmers, workers, residents, ethnic groups, or communities.
- Do not put countries, regions, organizations, governments, economies, infrastructure, resources, or services in affected_group.
- If absent, use "not_stated".
- Prefer "not_stated" over "not_applicable" when uncertain.

severity:
- "low": limited or mild impact.
- "medium": meaningful but not severe.
- "high": severe, large-scale, major, tremendous, or strongly harmful impact.
- "extreme": catastrophic, emergency, collapse-level, massive death toll, genocide, mass extermination, or massive impact.
- "not_stated": not grounded.
Do not infer severity from a number alone.
Use severity language from article_text when available.

modality:
- "asserted": stated as fact by the article or speaker.
- "reported": explicitly attributed to a source, report, authority, data source, or organization.
- "projected": expected, predicted, forecast, estimated, likely, planned, or warned about.
- "hedged": possible, uncertain, conditional, suspected, rumored, or unconfirmed.

grounding_quote:
- Exact contiguous quote from article_text.
- Must justify event_type.
- Prefer the shortest sufficient quote.
- event_location, event_time, affected_entity, and affected_group may come from nearby article_text if they clearly refer to the same event.
</field_rules>

<economic_event_rules>
For unemployment, inflation, recession, stagnation, debt, reduced trade, reduced investment, reduced remittances, sanctions, or economic dislocation:
- Extract only if the text states a concrete decline, disruption, or harm.
- Do not infer affected_group unless people or a population group is explicitly named.
- Do not infer severity from a number alone.
- Use "not_stated" for severity unless article_text explicitly characterizes the impact using severity language such as severe, tremendous, catastrophic, crisis, collapse, emergency, or similar.
</economic_event_rules>
