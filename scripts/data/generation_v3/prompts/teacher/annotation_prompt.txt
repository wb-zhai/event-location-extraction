<extraction_rules>
Extract only events explicitly supported by article text.
Extract one object per distinct event mention.
Do not merge separate events into one record.
If the same event is repeated without new event information, extract it once.
Do not extract background world knowledge.
Do not extract vague implications unless the text clearly states the event.
Do not create causal links.
Do not infer hidden events, IPC pillars, admin levels, coordinates, or event IDs.

Historical or background examples may be extracted if they are explicit risk-related events and can be grounded cleanly.
Do not extract a background example if it is only a causal analogy and the article does not state a concrete event matching the ontology.

If an article mentions a response, aid delivery, surgery, treatment, funding, policy action, or logistics action, do NOT extract it unless that response itself exactly matches an allowed ontology event label.

Prefer specific event mentions over broad themes:
- "shortages of food" -> food insecurity or food crisis
- "people fled their homes" -> flee or displacement or refugees, depending on wording
- "rainfall far below normal" -> drought or insufficient rainfall
- "armed clashes" -> conflict or civil strife, depending on wording
- "water stress", "water shortage", "decline in fresh water", or "decreased water availability" -> water availability or water distribution shortages
- "arsenic contamination", "salinity intrusion", "polluted groundwater", or "unsafe water" -> environmental degradation or ecological crisis if directly supported
</extraction_rules>

<event_type_disambiguation>
Do not label general disease burden or mortality from disease as "cholera outbreak" unless cholera is explicitly named. Use "epidemics affecting agriculture" only when livestock or crops are affected by an epidemic.
Use "environmental degradation" or "ecological crisis" for arsenic contamination, salinity, toxins, polluted groundwater, or contaminated water sources when directly supported.
Use "water availability" or "water distribution shortages" for water stress, water shortage, reduced water availability, reduced water flow, or depletion of renewable water unless a more specific allowed label is directly supported.
Use "harvest decline", "failed crops", or "harvest devastation" for explicit reduced harvests, crop yield drops, declining agricultural production, or production losses.
Use "food insecurity" or "food crisis" for explicit food shortages, food deficits, hunger increases, or insufficient access to food.
Use "displacement", "flee", or "refugees" when the text describes people being displaced, on the move, forced to move, or fleeing.
Use "humanitarian disaster" for large-scale suffering, casualties, or deaths when no more specific label applies.
</event_type_disambiguation>

<field_definitions>
document_relevance:
- "relevant" if at least one event is extracted.
- "not_relevant" if no event is extracted.

event_type:
- Must exactly match one allowed event_type value.
- If multiple labels seem possible, choose the most specific label directly supported by the grounding_quote.
- Remove the event if no allowed label is clearly supported.

event_location:
- The best event-specific location string explicitly grounded in the input text.
- The objective is to produce a place-name query that can later be processed by a geocoder such as Photon.
- Do not invent, complete, enrich, or disambiguate locations using world knowledge.
- Do not add parent locations such as country, province, district, or region unless they are part of the location phrase used in the article.
- Do not add ", Country" or other administrative context merely to help geocoding.
- Use only location names explicitly stated in the article text.
- The location does not need to appear inside grounding_quote, but it must appear somewhere in the article and must clearly scope the extracted event.
- If a location is mentioned elsewhere in the article but does not clearly scope the event, use "not_stated".
- Preserve the article's place-name wording as much as possible, with only minimal cleanup.

Location normalization rules:
- Remove vague spatial modifiers when a clean named place remains:
  - "southwestern part of Bangladesh" -> "Bangladesh"
  - "coastal areas of Bangladesh" -> "Bangladesh"
  - "areas around Kabul" -> "Kabul"
- Do not split coordinated region names incorrectly:
  - "South and South East Asia" -> "South Asia; South East Asia"
  - not "South; South East Asia"
- If the article lists multiple explicit event locations for the same event, include them as a semicolon-separated list in the order they appear:
  - "China, Nepal, India and Bangladesh" -> "China; Nepal; India; Bangladesh"
  - "Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Sindh, Punjab and Baluchistan regions" -> "Khyber Pakhtunkhwa; Sindh; Punjab; Baluchistan"
- Keep useful named geographic units when stated:
  - "Shandong province" -> "Shandong province"
  - "Satkhira district" -> "Satkhira district"
  - "Kathmandu Valley" -> "Kathmandu Valley"
  - "Indus River basin" -> "Indus River basin"
  - "Himalayan sub-region" -> "Himalayan sub-region"
- Do not replace a stated region with countries unless the countries are explicitly listed in the same sentence or immediate context.
- If both a broad region and specific places are explicitly listed for the same event, prefer the specific listed places.
- Do not use broad non-geocodable scopes as locations:
  - "world" -> "not_stated"
  - "globally" -> "not_stated"
  - "internationally" -> "not_stated"
  - "many regions" -> "not_stated"
  - "some regions" -> "not_stated"
  - "developing countries" -> "not_stated"
  - "the region" -> "not_stated" unless the named region is explicitly stated nearby.
- Use named regions only when they are explicitly stated:
  - "South Asia" -> "South Asia"
  - "South East Asia" -> "South East Asia"
  - "Middle East" -> "Middle East"
  - "Himalayan sub-region" -> "Himalayan sub-region"
- For migration or displacement:
  - Use the origin location for an origin crisis.
  - Use the destination location for an arrival, influx, or hosting event.
  - If both origin and destination are central and explicitly named, choose the location most directly tied to the extracted event.
- For river-basin or water-system events:
  - Use the named basin, river, glacier system, or water system if that is where the event occurs.
  - Examples: "Indus River basin", "Yellow River", "Himalayan glaciers".
- If no explicit named event location is stated, use "not_stated".

event_time:
- The most grounded event-specific time expression.
- Prefer normalized, machine-parseable values only when normalization is direct, lossless, and does not invent precision.
- Do not invent exact dates, months, years, ranges, start dates, or end dates.
- Do not convert vague expressions into precise dates.
- Use only:
  - the event-specific wording in the article;
  - the article publication date, if explicitly included in the input text;
  - simple calendar logic for relative expressions when the publication date is explicit.
- Do not use world knowledge, external sources, URLs, filenames, or metadata outside the input text.

Normalize only when direct:
- "from 1992 to 1996" -> "1992-1996"
- "between 1992 and 1996" -> "1992-1996"
- "in 2018" -> "2018"
- "in March 2018" -> "2018-03"
- "on 12 March 2018" -> "2018-03-12"
- "since March 2018" -> "2018-03-"
- "before 2018" -> "-2018"
- "after 2018" -> "2018-"
- "as of mid-2018" -> "2018"
- "early 2018", "mid-2018", "late 2018" -> "2018"

Keep vague but informative expressions when exact normalization would invent precision:
- "by the 2020s" -> "by the 2020s"
- "during the 2020s" -> "2020s"
- "middle of the century" -> "middle of the century"
- "by the middle of the century" -> "by the middle of the century"
- "in the next 20 years" -> "in the next 20 years" unless the article publication date is explicit and the exact range can be derived without ambiguity.
- "over the past ten years" -> "over the past ten years" unless the article publication date is explicit and the exact range can be derived without ambiguity.
- "in recent years" -> "in recent years"
- "currently" -> "currently"
- "now" -> "now"
- "still" -> "still"
- "ongoing" -> "ongoing"

Normalize relative expressions only when publication date is explicitly included in the input text:
- If publication date is "2018-07-15", "last month" -> "2018-06"
- If publication date is "2018-07-15", "this month" -> "2018-07"
- If publication date is "2018-07-15", "yesterday" -> "2018-07-14"
- If publication date is "2018-07-15", "today" -> "2018-07-15"
- If publication date is "2018-07-15", "tomorrow" -> "2018-07-16" only when the event is forecast or projected.

Do not over-normalize:
- "by the 2020s" must not become "2020-01-01/2029-12-31".
- "middle of the century" must not become "2050" unless the article explicitly says "2050".
- "past ten years" must not become a date range unless a publication date is explicitly present and the range is unambiguous.
- Do not add day or month precision when the article only gives a year, decade, season, or vague period.

If no event-specific time is stated, use "not_stated".

time_status:
- "past" if the event clearly happened before the article's publication/reporting moment and is completed or historical.
- "ongoing" if the event is continuing, current, rising, still happening, or affecting people at the article's publication/reporting moment.
- "forecast" if the event is expected, projected, warned about, likely, predicted, or planned to happen after the article's publication/reporting moment.
- "not_stated" if the article does not provide enough information.
- Base this only on article wording, the explicitly provided publication date in the input text, and event_time.
- Do not infer time_status from world knowledge.

affected_entity:
- The thing, system, asset, or livelihood affected.
- Examples: "food and medicine", "healthcare system", "water supply", "maize crop", "roads", "aid supplies".
- If absent, use "not_stated".
- Prefer "not_stated" over "not_applicable" unless the field truly cannot apply to the event.

affected_group:
- The people or population group affected.
- Examples: "farmers", "children", "refugees", "pastoralists", "Venezuelan migrants".
- If absent, use "not_stated".
- Prefer "not_stated" over "not_applicable" unless the field truly cannot apply to the event.
- For humanitarian, disaster, displacement, food, water, health, and conflict events, affected_group usually makes sense; if the article does not name the group, use "not_stated".

severity:
- "low" for limited or mild impact.
- "medium" for meaningful but not severe impact.
- "high" for severe or large-scale impact.
- "extreme" for catastrophic, emergency, collapse-level, or massive impact.
- "not_stated" if severity cannot be grounded from the article.
- Base severity only on article wording and stated figures. Never infer severity from world knowledge.

modality:
- "asserted" if the article states the event as a fact, or attributes it to a source, authority, official, report, or organization.
- "projected" if the event is expected, predicted, forecast, estimated, warned about, possible, uncertain, suspected, conditional, or explicitly not confirmed.

grounding_quote:
- Must be one exact, contiguous span copied from the article.
- Must be sufficient to justify the event_type.
- Prefer the shortest local quote that clearly supports the event_type.
- The grounding_quote does not need to include event_location if the location is explicitly stated elsewhere in the article and clearly scopes the event.
- The grounding_quote does not need to include event_time if the time is explicitly stated elsewhere in the article and clearly scopes the event.
- Do not paraphrase.
</field_definitions>
